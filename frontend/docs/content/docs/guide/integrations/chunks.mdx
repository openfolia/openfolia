`GET /chunks` gives a retrieval pipeline the content as ready-made chunks, with the metadata to
filter on and a hash, so you embed a chunk again only when it changed. It is part of the
[Delivery API](/api/delivery) and needs `content.read`. To keep an index in step over time, read
the [change feed](/guide/integrations/change-feed).

## What a chunk is

- **One chunk per module and language**, the module as Markdown: the title as a `#` heading,
  sections as deeper headings, lists, numbered steps, tables, safety messages and notes as quotes
  headed by their signal word, images as `*Figure:*` lines with their alternative text.
- **One more chunk per FAQ entry**, after its module's: the question as heading, then its answer.
- **Module references** are put in, but only modules the reader may see and no more restricted
  than the including module, so a chunk's `audience` holds for all of its text.
- **Variant blocks** are quotes headed by `**when**` and their condition. With
  `filter[variant]=model=large` they are resolved for that variant instead.

A chunk's `id` is `<lang>/<moduleId>` (an FAQ entry's `<lang>/<moduleId>/<blockId>`). Its
`attributes` hold `moduleId`, `lang`, `title`, `moduleType`, `audience`, `facets`, `keywords`,
`manuals` (the manuals that include it), `conditions`, `text`, `sourceHash` and `contentHash` (the
SHA-256 of `text`). `meta` holds `commit`, `sources` and `release` (the newest release that holds
the module). Changing only a file's formatting changes neither `text` nor `contentHash`.

## Read every chunk

1. Ask for the first page at `main`:

   ```sh
   curl -gs -i -H "Authorization: Bearer $TOKEN" \
     "https://docs.example.com/api/chunks?ref=main&page[size]=200"
   ```

2. Keep the commit the answer names in `X-Ccms-Commit`, and read every further page at
   `?ref=<that commit>`, so all pages come from one commit. A consumer's token may read that
   commit only while `main` (or a tag) still points at it: if `main` moves on during the read, a
   later page answers 404 `not-found`; start again at step 1. To read one release whatever comes
   after it, read every page at its release tag instead.
3. Store each chunk by `id`. Embed it only when its `contentHash` is new.
4. Filter retrieval on `audience`, `facets`, `manuals`, `lang` and `meta.release`.

Next time, `filter[changedSince]=<the commit you kept>` lists only the chunks that may differ since.
It does not list deletions or what left the reader's scope: follow the change feed for those, or
read everything again now and then (and after each release, for `meta.release`).

**Filters**, all optional: `filter[module]`, `filter[lang]`, `filter[manual]`, `filter[variant]`,
`filter[changedSince]`, and the module filters of [search](/guide/integrations/search)
(`filter[type]`, `filter[audience]`, `filter[facet]`, `filter[keyword]`). A chunk outside the
reader's scope is never listed, counted or put in. Drafts (any ref other than `main` and tags)
need `content.read.draft`, which a consumer's token does not hold.

## Modules left out as too large

Module references that multiply can make a module too large to cut. A module's chunks, its FAQ
entries' included, are held to the limits a manual has: 500,000 content nodes, 12,000,000
characters, 1,000,000 table cells (rows × columns), 100 levels deep. Past one, only that module is
left out:

- it has no chunk, and the others are served as usual;
- `meta.leftOut` names it, with `moduleId`, `lang` and `reason` (`size` or `depth`), only to
  readers who may see it;
- the change feed carries the release without its chunks;
- before the release, the module's checks show a blocking `expansion-too-large`, so the writer
  can fix it (see [Fix what the checks find](/guide/checks/fixing-issues)).

## A commit's chunks too large together

Each module may be under the limits while many of them together are not (a hundred small modules
that each refer to one that multiplies). So the chunks of a commit, as one reader sees them,
share one budget: four times the content they are made from, plus room for 32 modules at the
limits of one. Past it, every request for that commit's chunks, each page and each filter, is
422 `chunk-set-too-large`:

- `meta` names the budget, and `meta.largest` the up to five modules (among those the reader may
  see) whose chunks expanded the most, with `moduleId`, `lang`, `chars` and `nodes`;
- it is that commit's and that reader's only: other commits, and readers who may not see the
  modules that multiply, are served as usual;
- the change feed skips such a release;
- before the release, a revision's checks show a blocking `chunk-set-too-large` at those modules
  (see [References, images and terms](/guide/checks/references)). The fix is in the content: refer
  to fewer modules, over fewer paths.
