These checks read what a module says: the words of its paragraphs, titles, list items, alternative
texts, safety messages and other translatable text. Code, literal blocks and passthrough blocks are
never read. They are warnings unless your project's configuration makes them blocking.

## Terms

The terms of your [terminology](/guide/writing/terminology) name the variants that must not be used, per
language.

- **Forbidden term**: the text uses a forbidden variant of an approved term, in the module's
  language. It is matched as a whole word and in any case, without word endings: “loader” is found
  in “Plug in the Loader”, not in “loaders”. The details say what to write instead, the term's
  preferred variant, in the case that was found.
- The same check reports a **deprecated term**: any variant of a term marked deprecated, with its
  replacement's preferred variant when the term names one. It is always a warning.

A proposed term is not in force yet and checks nothing. A forbidden term is blocking when the
configuration says so:

```yaml
terminology:
  forbiddenIsBlocking: true
```

## Style rules

Your house style, as rules in `openfolia.yaml`, per language:

```yaml
styleRules:
  en:
    maxSentenceWords: 25
    rules:
      - id: in-order-to
        words: in order to
        message: Write “to”.
        replacement: to
      - id: utilise
        pattern: '\butili[sz]e\b'
        message: Write “use”.
        replacement: use
        severity: blocking
```

- **Style rule**: the text matches a rule. A rule is a phrase (`words`, matched as whole words in
  any case) or a pattern (`pattern`), with the message the writer reads and, optionally, what to
  write instead. A rule is a warning unless its `severity` is `blocking`.
- The same check reports a sentence longer than `maxSentenceWords` words.

A pattern is a small part of regular expressions: characters, `.`, classes such as `[a-z]` and
`\w`, groups, `|`, `?`, `*`, `+`, counts up to 20, `^`, `$` and `\b`. It is matched in time that
grows with the text only, so no pattern can hold up the checks. A pattern outside that part
(back-references, lookaround) is refused when the configuration is saved, and says why. The
[`openfolia.yaml` reference](/reference/project-config) lists every key.

**Writing not checked** (warning): a module's text is longer than the checks read (1,000,000
characters), has more findings than they list (1,000), or took the style rules too long. What comes
after was not checked: split the module, or fix the first findings.

## While you write

The module editor runs the same checks as you type, in the module's language, on the terms and
style rules of the revision you edit. They run when you pause, on the text you changed only.

- A finding is **underlined**: dotted, thicker for a blocking one, with a warning icon beside its
  paragraph. Point at it, or put the caret in it and press **Alt+Enter**, to open its card: what is
  wrong, the note of the term, and what to write instead. **Replace** puts the fix in, as one step
  that **Ctrl+Z** (**Cmd+Z** on a Mac) takes back. **Ignore here** hides that finding in that text
  until you close the module; the checks before review and release still report it.
- The **Suggestions** tab of the side panel lists every finding under **Writing checks**, with
  **Replace**, **Show** (selects the text) and **Ignore here**.
- Typing the first three letters of an approved term's preferred variant shows the rest in grey:
  **Tab** puts it in, linked to the term; **Escape** dismisses it. Typing an allowed variant in full
  offers to link it the same way. To insert a term by searching, choose **Term** in the **/** menu
  or under **Insert** in the toolbar.
- A term link is underlined thinly. Pointing at it, or resting the caret in it, shows its
  definition.

The suggestions of the [style review](/guide/writing/style-review) are marked in the text too
(dashed), with **Apply** and **Dismiss** in their card.

## In CI

The same checks run on a content repository from the command line, for a pipeline:

```sh
pnpm nx run api:check-content -- --content path/to/content --ref main
```

It prints one line per check and exits 1 when any check is blocking, 0 with warnings only.
`--manual <id>` checks one manual, `--format json` prints JSON.
