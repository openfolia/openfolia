A snippet is a short piece of wording you use again and again, such as a standard warning or a
sentence about the warranty. Snippets are modules of a type your project marks as a snippet type.

## Insert a snippet

1. Put the caret where the snippet should go.
2. Choose **Insert**, then **Snippet**. Or type `/snippet` and press <kbd>Enter</kbd>.
3. In **Insert snippet**, type in **Search snippets** and choose one.
4. Choose how to insert it:
   - **As reference**: kept in sync. A change to the snippet shows everywhere it is used, and it
     is translated once.
   - **As copy**: its text is copied here, and you can change it freely. Nothing links back to the
     snippet.
5. Choose **Insert**.

## Accept a suggested snippet

While you type, the editor watches for the start of a snippet's wording. When it finds one, it
shows the rest of the snippet in grey after the caret.

- <kbd>Tab</kbd> inserts it as a reference.
- <kbd>Shift</kbd> <kbd>Tab</kbd> inserts it as a copy.
- <kbd>Esc</kbd> dismisses it. Or just type on.

## Save blocks as a snippet

1. Select the blocks: one, or a few paragraphs or a list. A snippet holds no headings and no
   module references.
2. Choose **Save as snippet** in the toolbar that appears over the selection. For a single block,
   the block actions menu (the grip to the left) has it too.
3. Enter a **Title**. The **ID** is made from it.
4. Choose the **Snippet type** if your project has more than one.
5. Choose **Save snippet**.

The snippet is saved in your revision, and a reference to it takes the place of the blocks.
