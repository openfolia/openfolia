A procedure is a task in numbered steps. Each step can hold more than its text: an image, a note, a
safety notice, the result it leads to.

## Write a procedure

1. Put the caret where the procedure should go.
2. Type <kbd>/</kbd>, then `proc`, and choose **Procedure**.
3. Type the first step. Press <kbd>Enter</kbd> for the next one.
4. Press <kbd>Enter</kbd> in an empty last step to leave the procedure.

A procedure's title is optional. Choose **Add title** at its top to give it one, such as "Replace the
fuse".

![A procedure with a title and three steps, an image under the second step](/images/guide/en/writing-procedure.webp)

## Sub-steps

- <kbd>Tab</kbd> in a step makes it a sub-step of the step before.
- <kbd>Shift</kbd> <kbd>Tab</kbd> in a sub-step brings it back up. The sub-steps
  after it come with it.

The first step cannot become a sub-step: there is no step before it.

## Say what a step leads to

1. Put the caret in the step.
2. Type <kbd>/</kbd> and choose **Step result**.
3. Type the result, such as "The cover opens."

The result stands under the step's text, marked **Result**. A step has one result at most.

## Put an image in a step

1. Put the caret in the step's text.
2. Choose **Insert**, or type <kbd>/</kbd>, and choose **Image**. See [Images](/guide/writing/images).

The image goes under the step's text, in that step. A note, a safety notice, a table or code goes
in a step the same way. A page break cannot: it goes after the procedure.

## Move steps

Put the caret in a step and press <kbd>Alt</kbd> <kbd>↑</kbd> or <kbd>Alt</kbd> <kbd>↓</kbd>, or drag it by its
grip. A step moves among the steps of its own procedure. An image in a step moves within its step,
or into the steps around it. See [Move, copy and delete blocks](/guide/writing/arranging-blocks).
