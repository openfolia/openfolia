## The page for this screen

Every screen has a question mark beside its title. Choose it to open the page of this user guide
that explains the screen, in a new tab and in the language you use the editor in.

The account menu has the same link: open the menu at the foot of the sidebar and choose **Help
for this page**.

## Find a guide page

The search palette finds pages of this guide too, by their titles and summaries.

1. Press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> <kbd>K</kbd> (<kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>K</kbd> on a Mac).
2. Type what you want to do, for example `xliff`.
3. The pages that match are under **Help**, marked **User guide**. Open one with <kbd>Enter</kbd>.

## Every keyboard shortcut

Press <kbd>?</kbd> anywhere outside a text field, or choose **Keyboard shortcuts** in the account
menu. The list shows each shortcut where it works, with the keys of your computer.

Close it with <kbd>Esc</kbd>.

The [keyboard shortcuts](/guide/reference/shortcuts) page lists the same shortcuts for Windows,
Linux and macOS.

## The tour

The first time you open the editor, a short tour shows you what OpenFolia is for and walks you
through your first edit. Each step asks you to do one thing and moves on when you have done it.
It lights up the part of the screen it is about, dims the rest, and points at it with a small
arrow. The dots at the bottom show how far you are.

1. **Write it once, publish it everywhere**: what modules are for. Choose **Show me how**, or
   **Not now** to skip the tour.
2. **Open a module**: open the module the step lights up in the list, or any other.
3. **Change a sentence**: click into the text and type. When no revision is open, the tour starts
   one for you, named after the module. Your change is saved as you type, in your revision: your
   draft, kept apart from the published content until it is reviewed.
4. **Your own revision**: the revision menu at the top right, which shows the revision your
   change is in. The published content stays as it is until your revision is reviewed and
   merged. In this menu you switch between revisions and the published content, or start a new
   revision.
5. **Send it for review**: where you submit your revision once you are done. A reviewer approves
   it, a publisher merges it into the published content, and the next release takes it to the
   manuals. **Done** ends the tour; [Your first edit](/guide/getting-started/first-edit) has the
   rest of the way.

![The tour's Open a module step beside the first row of the content list](/images/guide/en/tour.webp)

A step's main button does the step for you too, from the keyboard for example: **Open this
module** opens the lit module, **Start typing** puts the cursor at the end of the lit sentence.

If you can only read the content, the tour shows you around instead: what modules are for, how to
open one, and where the manuals are. If you may also read revisions in progress, it shows you the
revision menu too: you read the published content, or choose a revision to read its changes. It
changes nothing.

If you take the tour again, it goes on in the revision you edited in your last tour, if that is still a
draft of yours, rather than starting another.

- If you leave the tour's way (another screen, a click elsewhere, the search palette), it pauses
  and comes back at the same step. Back on a screen where the step can be shown, it carries on.
  It keeps its place when you reload the page.
- **×**, **Not now** or <kbd>Esc</kbd> ends it for good. <kbd>Esc</kbd> works wherever you are
  on the page.
- <kbd>Tab</kbd> moves between the buttons of the step.
- <kbd>→</kbd> does what the step's main button does, and <kbd>←</kbd> goes back
  a step, while the step or nothing on the page has the focus (in the text, the
  arrows move the cursor).

On a phone or a narrow window, the steps about the sidebar point at the menu button that opens
it. If you resize the window during the tour, each step finds its place again.

The tour comes once. To see it again, open the account menu and choose **Take the tour**. It
starts even when tips are off, and leaves **Show tips** as it was.

## Tips

The first time you come to a part of the editor, a tip may explain it: a short note beside the
button or list it is about, with **Learn more** when this guide has a page on it. Each tip shows
once.

![A tip beside New module on the Content page](/images/guide/en/tip.webp)

- **Got it** or <kbd>Esc</kbd> closes it for good.
- **Turn off tips** turns off every tip and the tour.

A tip never opens while a dialog, a menu or the search palette is open, or while you type in a
field: it waits until you are done.

The tips are:

- **Start a module**, beside **New module** on the Content page, if you may write.
- **See it as it prints**, beside **Preview** in the module editor.
- **Only for some products?**, beside the header of the first variant block you see in a module, if
  you may edit it and the project has variant attributes. It says that text for some products goes
  into a [variant block](/guide/variants/variant-blocks), and that Preview shows the module as each
  variant.
- **Your revisions and your reviews**, beside the filters on the Review page.
- **Hear back on your revisions**, beside the bell, once you have a revision of your own.
- **Find anything**, beside the search button.

Your choices are kept with your account, so they follow you to another browser or computer.

### Turn tips on or off

Open the account menu and choose **Show tips**: the check mark shows whether tips are on. **Reset
tips** turns them on and shows every tip again, also the ones you closed.

## When there are no help links

An administrator can point the help links at another copy of this guide, or turn them off for an
installation with no way out to the internet (`CCMS_DOCS_URL`, [self-hosting](/self-hosting)).
Without them, the question marks and **Help for this page** are not there, and the search
palette finds no guide pages. **Keyboard shortcuts** is always there.
