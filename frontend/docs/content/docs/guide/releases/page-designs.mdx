A page design sets how a manual's PDF looks: the page size, the title page, the colours, the
fonts, the running header and footer, headings, tables and safety notices. Designs are content:
you change them in a revision of your own, and they apply once it is merged. See
[Almost everything is content](/guide/getting-started/content).

## Open the page designs

1. Start or choose a draft revision of your own.
2. Choose **Admin** in the sidebar, then **Page design**.

The list shows every design with its ID, its layout and how many problems it has. Changing a
design needs a role that may edit all content; everyone else sees it read-only, with why.

![The page designs list with the default design above it](/images/guide/en/page-design-list.webp)

## Make a design

1. Choose **New design**.
2. Enter a **Name**. The **ID** is filled in from it; it cannot be changed later.
3. Turn on **Use as the default design** to print every manual without a design of its own with
   it.
4. Choose **Create design**. The design opens.

## Change a design

Choose a design in the list. Each change is saved in your revision at once; **Undo** takes back
the last one. The sections, from top to bottom:

- **Layout:** how the title page is laid out: **Classic**, **Image-led** or **Minimal**.
- **Page:** the **Page size** (**A4**, **US Letter** or **A5**), the margins, and **Printed
  double-sided**.
- **Logo**, **Colours** and **Fonts**: see [Fonts and cover images](/guide/releases/page-design-assets).
- **Title page fields:** which data the title page shows, in which order. Drag a row by its grip.
- **Title and back page text:** text of your own on the title page (below).
- **Back page:** the imprint, last or on the inside of the title page (below).
- **Running header and footer:** what each inner page shows at the left, centre and right, for
  example the **Manual title** and the **Page number**.
- **Headings**, **Body text**, **Tables** and **Steps and notes**.
- **Safety notices:** the **Safety standard**, **ANSI Z535** or **ISO 3864**. Their colours,
  signal words and symbols are **Fixed by the standard**.

![A page design: its name, layout and page beside the preview](/images/guide/en/page-design-editor.webp)

## The preview

**Preview** shows the title page and an inner page of a real manual with the design, as you
change it. Choose the **Manual**, **Variant** and **Language** above it, and page through with the
arrows. **Layout warnings** below say when something does not fit, for example a running header
cut short; **Everything fits.** when all is well.

## Text on the title page

**Title and back page text** holds free text for the layout's places, such as **Above the title**
or **Title page foot**:

1. Choose the **Language** at the top. The source language comes first.
2. Open a place and type. To add manual data, such as the product version, choose **Insert**,
   then **Placeholder**, or type <kbd>{"{"}</kbd>.
3. For another language, choose it and write its own text. A language without its own text
   prints the source language's: the number beside each language counts the texts it lacks.

![The title page text in English, with a placeholder, beside the preview](/images/guide/en/page-design-regions.webp)

## The back page and imprint

**Back page** prints the imprint: the manufacturer's details, the document number, the release
date and a copyright line.

1. Turn on **Print a back page**.
2. Under **Where it goes**, choose **Last page** (after the content; a left page when printed
   double-sided) or **Inside the cover** (on the back of the title page, before the contents).
3. Turn **Show the document number** and **Show the release date** on or off.
4. Enter the **Copyright line**. With several languages, choose a language above the field to
   give it a line of its own; a language without one prints the source language's.

The back page's own text is under **Title and back page text**. The release and its date are
filled in when a release's PDF is made, for example **2.3** and **November 20, 2026**; the
PDF preview and a download before a release leave them out.

![The back page section with the manufacturer's details from the project settings](/images/guide/en/page-design-back-page.webp)

### The manufacturer's details

The manufacturer is set once for the project, not per design: choose **Admin**, **Project
settings**, then **Manufacturer**, in a revision of your own. Enter the **Name**, the **Address**
(one line per row), the **Contact** and the **Website** (a full address such as
`https://example.com`). Every design's back page prints them, and so does any text with a
manufacturer placeholder, such as **Manufacturer name**.

Turn on **Warn before a release without manufacturer details** to have every manual's checks
report **Manufacturer details missing** while the details are empty.

![The manufacturer's details in the project settings](/images/guide/en/settings-manufacturer.webp)

## Small pages: A5 and leaflets

For short documents, such as a [safety leaflet](/guide/manuals/safety-leaflet), choose **A5**,
the **Minimal** layout, smaller type and margins. The sample content's **Safety leaflet** design is
one. A safety notice is always kept whole on one page. One taller than a whole page is broken
across pages, and the manual's checks report **Safety message split across pages**.

## Which design a manual uses

- The default: **Default design** above the list. Manuals without a design of their own are
  printed with it. Changing it needs a role that may change the project settings; in the
  published content, **Change in a draft** starts a revision for it.
- A manual's own: in the manual's **Settings**, under **Page design**. **Project default (…)** follows the
  default and names it, for example **Project default (Built-in design)**.

The PDF preview and a release's files name the design used, for example **Design: Built-in design**. A
manual naming a design that is not there is printed with the built-in design, and its checks
report **Unknown page design**.

A design that the default or a manual still uses cannot be deleted. Choose another design there
first.

## Own templates

An organisation can lay its PDFs out with a Typst template of its own instead of the layout's.
A template is a folder in the content repository, `templates/<name>/`, with its entry file
`manual.typ`. It is content: you change its files in a revision of your own, in the editor (see
[Own templates](/guide/releases/page-design-templates)) or by pushing them to the repository,
and they are reviewed with the revision that changes them.

1. In **Layout**, choose the template under **Template**. **The layout's template** uses none.
2. The preview shows the manual with it. The layout still decides the places on the title page.

**Where to start.** The example template is the **Classic** layout as files of its own:
`manual.typ` (the title page), `design.typ` (the design's settings, the running header and
footer, the back page) and `default.typ` (the style of each block). Copied into
`templates/<name>/`, it prints exactly what **Classic** prints; change what you need. The sample
content has it as `templates/example/`.

**What a template defines.** The functions the generated manual calls: `manual` (a show rule over
the whole document), `anchor`, `safety`, `admonition`, `procedure`, `ui`, `code-span`, `kbd`,
`xref` and `unconverted`, and if it wants, `attachment`, `header-cell`, `faq`, `term`,
`glossary-entry`, `callout-image` and `callout-ref`. It reads the design's settings with
`json(bytes(sys.inputs.at("openfolia-design")))` and the design's texts from the `"openfolia-design"`
state. Every PDF must still meet PDF/UA-1: an image needs alternative text or is marked as
decoration.

**What a template can read.** Only the files in its own folder (with relative paths such as
`#import "parts.typ"` or `#image("logo.svg", alt: "…")`), the design's logo, fonts and images
and the manual's images. Never another template, a module, the project settings, the server's
files or the network; Typst packages cannot be imported. A template that runs too long or needs
too much memory is stopped.

**A template with an error.** The preview shows where: the file, line and column, and why. The
PDF is not made with it, and the checks report **Page design template does not compile** as
blocking, at each manual that uses the design and, in a revision that changes the design or its
template, at the design itself: such a revision cannot be merged until the template is fixed.
Until then the published template stays in use. A design naming a template that is not in the
repository is printed with its layout's template, and the checks report **Unknown page design
template**.
