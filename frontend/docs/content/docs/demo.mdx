title: Try the demo
description: Start OpenFolia from a fresh clone with one command, and walk through writing, review and releases as three demo people.

The demo runs the editor and the API on your machine, with the documentation of an invented car
and three demo people. It needs no Docker and no database server, and it leaves your own set-up
alone. To run OpenFolia for real, see [Getting started](/getting-started) and
[Self-hosting](/self-hosting).

## What you need

- Node 24 and pnpm 11 (`corepack enable` picks the version the repository names).
- Git 2.40 or newer.
- Ports 3290 and 4390 free.

## Start it

In a clone of the repository:

```sh
pnpm install
pnpm demo:start
```

The first start builds the API and the editor, which takes a few minutes. Then it makes the
content and the database, cuts two releases and renders their PDFs, which takes about a minute.
It ends with an address:

```
Open:     http://127.0.0.1:4390/__start?lang=en&user=demo-author&theme=light
```

Open it in your browser. It signs you in as the author and shows **Content**.

| Command                     | What it does                                                    |
| --------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `pnpm demo:start --lang de` | The editor in German. What the demo writes is in German too.    |
| `pnpm demo:reset`           | Everything made again, as after a start. Your changes are gone. |
| `pnpm demo:stop`            | Stops it and removes its content and its database.              |
| `pnpm demo:status`          | Says whether it runs, and its address.                          |

## What is in it

- **The content:** 32 modules of an owner's manual and a service booklet, in English and German,
  and partly in French. Three manuals, built for variants of body (hatchback, estate), drive
  (petrol, electric) and market (left- and right-hand drive).
- **Two releases**, 1.0 and 1.1, with their PDFs. 1.1 changed one tyre pressure.
- **A revision in review**, "Revise changing a wheel", with comments from the reviewer.
- **Three people:** an author, a reviewer (who may also write) and an admin. The account menu
  shows their names.

## The walkthrough

### 1. As the author: change a module

1. In **Content**, choose **Setting the clock**. It is **Released** and read-only: you read the
   published content.
2. Open the revision menu at the top right, which shows **Published content**, and choose **New
   revision**. Enter a **Title**, for example "Explain the clock after parking", and choose
   **Start**.
3. The module is now **Locked by you**. Click at the end of its first paragraph and type a
   sentence. The header shows **Saved**.
4. Choose **Review** in the sidebar, then **Drafts**, then your revision. Choose **Submit for
   review**.

### 2. As the reviewer: review and approve

1. Open the account menu (your name at the bottom of the sidebar) and, under **Switch user**,
   choose the reviewer.
2. Choose **Review**. Under **Waiting**, choose **Revise changing a wheel**.
3. Read the changes **Inline**, **Side by side** or **Rendered**, and the comments on the steps.
   Reply to one, or add your own with **Comment**.
4. Choose **Approve**. Your own revision from step 1 is waiting there too: approve it as well.

### 3. As the admin: merge and look at the releases

1. Switch to the admin.
2. Choose **Review**, then **Approved**, then a revision, and choose **Merge**. Its change is now
   in the published content.
3. Choose **Releases**, then **1.1**, then **Files and changes**. **Changes** lists what changed
   since 1.0. Under **Files**, choose **Download** for an estate's PDF of the **Owner's manual**
   or the **Service booklet**: its tyre pressure table shows the new rear pressure.
4. Choose **Translations**: the French column shows what is missing or out of date.
5. Choose **Manuals**: the owner's manual, its modules in order, and its outputs per language.

Cut a release of your own with **New release** on **Releases**; see
[Cut a release](/guide/releases/overview).

## What it shows, and what it doesn't

**It shows** the product as it is: the real API and editor, content in a real Git repository,
revisions, review and merge, releases with their PDFs, translations and variants.

**It doesn't show:**

- **Signing in.** The demo people are chosen, not signed in: no password, no code by email, no
  sign-in with your organisation's account. Whoever reaches the demo may act as anyone, the admin
  too: it listens on 127.0.0.1 only, so don't forward its ports or put a proxy in front of it. See
  [Authentication modes](/guide/self-hosting/authentication).
- **Bringing your own content.** No import or migration from another system; the content is the
  demo's own.
- **Keeping anything.** The database runs in memory. A stop or a reset removes everything you
  changed.
- **Running it on a server.** No containers, backups or reverse proxy. See
  [Self-hosting](/self-hosting).

What the demo content holds, and how its videos are made, is in `docs/demo-videos.md` in the
repository.
